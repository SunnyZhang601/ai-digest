**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 96: Nebius CTO on Racing the Hyperscalers Before AWS Wakes Up**
The Takeaway: The only real bottleneck in the AI buildout isn't power, chips, or HBM, it's your organization's ability to execute across all of those shortages at once, which makes an experienced project manager the scarcest resource in the industry.

Nebius CTO Danila (who has been with the company since before it was called Nebius, through its split from Yandex) makes a case that the AI cloud market is far from settled. Nebius deliberately rejects the "neocloud" label, which he hates "with a passion" now that 300 to 500 companies claim it, and positions itself against AWS and Google Cloud directly, despite revenue that is roughly 1% of AWS. The strategic bet is that hyperscalers are structurally distracted: they must juggle enormous internal consumption for training and research against renting capacity out, and retrofitting infrastructure built for other workloads is a massive investment that has to be justified by a business that isn't big enough yet.

The most counterintuitive point is about inference. Danila is blunt that the industry has barely optimized it: "I think as an industry, we didn't optimize shit." Running open-weight models as published gives underwhelming results, and he sees far more upside in model pruning and selective compression than in the much-hyped prefill/decode disaggregation, which he says everyone talks about and no one does properly. His reasoning is sharp: 95% of Nebius customers don't run vanilla open source models, they run fine-tuned ones scoped to a narrow domain, which means you may not need such a large model at all. For real enterprise AI, predictability of outcome and real seconds to get there matter more than cost.

On the buildout, he lists the shortages (GPUs, CPUs, NVMe, transceivers, transformers with multi-year lead times, facilities, electricity, workforce) and concludes they all collapse into one constraint: execution. On talent, he is notably conservative about autonomous agents, insisting that whatever an agent produced is still your pull request and you're still the one on call, and that fully autonomous agents won't touch core services for a while. His biggest admitted mistake: underestimating scale, since a 30 megawatt facility used to be serious and now sometimes isn't enough for a single client.

The Takeaway：AI 基建唯一的真正瓶颈不是电力、芯片或 HBM，而是你的组织同时应对所有这些短缺的执行能力，这让有经验的项目经理成为行业里最稀缺的资源。

Nebius CTO Danila（在公司还叫 Nebius 之前就在，经历了从 Yandex 分拆的全过程）论证 AI cloud 市场远未定局。Nebius 刻意拒绝"neocloud"这个标签，他现在对这个词"深恶痛绝"，因为已经有 300 到 500 家公司这么自称，而 Nebius 直接把自己定位成 AWS 和 Google Cloud 的对手，尽管收入大约只有 AWS 的 1%。其战略赌注是：hyperscaler 在结构上被分散了注意力，它们必须在庞大的内部训练和研究算力消耗与对外出租容量之间反复权衡，而为其他 workload 建的基础设施做改造是一笔巨额投资，需要一个还不够大的业务来证明其合理性。

最反直觉的观点关于 inference。Danila 直言这个行业几乎没怎么优化过它："我认为作为一个行业，我们啥也没优化。"直接跑开源权重模型的结果很一般，他认为 model pruning 和选择性压缩的潜力远大于被热炒的 prefill/decode 分离，后者是"所有人都在谈，没人真正做好"。他的推理很锋利：Nebius 95% 的客户跑的不是原版开源模型，而是针对狭窄领域 fine-tune 过的模型，这意味着你可能根本不需要那么大的模型。对真正的企业级 AI 来说，结果的可预测性和拿到结果的实际秒数，比成本更重要。

谈到基建，他列出一堆短缺（GPU、CPU、NVMe、光模块、交期长达数年的变压器、机房、电力、人力），然后得出结论：它们最终都归结为一个约束，执行能力。在团队管理上，他对自主 agent 明显保守，坚持 agent 产出的东西仍然是你的 pull request，半夜出问题还是你 on call，完全自主的 agent 一段时间内不会碰核心服务。他承认的最大失误是低估了规模：30 兆瓦的机房过去算很可观，现在有时连一个客户都不够用。
