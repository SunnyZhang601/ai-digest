**No Priors: Taking on ServiceNow: Coding Agents the Rest of Us with Serval Co-Founder and CEO Jake Stauch**
https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast

The Takeaway: In the enterprise, the guardrails are the product. Serval co-founder and CEO Jake Stauch argues that the winning AI company in enterprise software will not be the one with the smartest model, but the one that owns the boundary between autonomy and permission.

Stauch spent years at Verkada selling to IT teams and kept hearing the same two answers when he asked what they spent the most money on and what they hated most: ServiceNow. He and co-founder Alex set out from day one, in Alex's living room with two people, to build "the largest enterprise software company of all time" and beat ServiceNow directly rather than building a thin layer on top of it or going downmarket. The bet that made it possible: code is the right way to express automation, and coding agents would get good enough that anyone could write it. Drag-and-drop workflow builders, in his view, are "too complex for nontechnical users, and it's annoyingly abstracted for technical users."

The insight that reframes the whole company is about permissions. As agents get more capable, enterprises will shrink every employee down to true least privilege, because the blast radius of any slightly elevated access expands enormously once agents can act on it. That makes the exception-granting layer, with approvals, audit trails, and tickets, more essential, not less.

"Serval is the boundary. Serval is the guardrails. We are the way that a large enterprise with a lot of rules and approvals and process and politics can adopt these tools safely and comfortably at scale."

His advice on selling into this market is refreshingly blunt: you have to convince customers that adopting you gets them promoted, not just that it won't get them fired. At Fox Corporation, the person who led the company-wide migration was nominated MVP of the entire company. On the "SaaSpocalypse," he's a skeptic at the core: point solutions and middleware will consolidate, but systems of record like ERP, HRIS, ITSM, and CRM aren't disappearing, they're being replaced by a new generation.

**No Priors：Taking on ServiceNow: Coding Agents the Rest of Us，对话 Serval 联合创始人兼 CEO Jake Stauch**
https://www.youtube.com/@NoPriorsPodcast

核心要点：在企业级市场，护栏本身就是产品。Serval 联合创始人兼 CEO Jake Stauch 认为，企业软件领域最终胜出的 AI 公司不会是模型最聪明的那家，而是掌握“自主性”与“权限”之间边界的那家。

Stauch 在 Verkada 工作多年，向 IT 团队销售产品，每次问他们“钱花得最多的地方”和“最讨厌的东西”时，得到的答案都是 ServiceNow。他和联合创始人 Alex 从第一天起，就在 Alex 的客厅里、只有两个人的时候，立志打造“史上最大的企业软件公司”，直接正面击败 ServiceNow，而不是在它之上做一层薄薄的封装，也不是先打低端市场。支撑这个赌注的判断是：代码才是表达自动化的正确方式，而 coding agent 会变得足够好，让任何人都能写代码。在他看来，拖拽式工作流搭建器“对非技术用户来说太复杂，对技术用户来说又抽象得让人恼火”。

真正重塑这家公司的洞察是关于权限的。随着 agent 能力增强，企业会把每个员工的权限收缩到真正的最小权限，因为一旦 agent 能据此行动，任何稍微超出常规的访问权限所带来的破坏半径都会急剧扩大。这让那个负责授予例外的层（带审批、审计追踪和工单）变得更重要，而不是更不重要。

“Serval 就是边界。Serval 就是护栏。我们让一个有着大量规则、审批、流程和政治的大型企业，能够安全、舒适地规模化采用这些工具。”

他对如何向这个市场销售的建​​议非常直白：你必须让客户相信，采用你不仅能让他们不被开除，还能让他们升职。在 Fox Corporation，主导全公司迁移的那个人被提名为整个公司的 MVP。对于“SaaS 末日论”，他在核心判断上是怀疑派：点解决方案和中间件会整合，但 ERP、HRIS、ITSM、CRM 这类系统记录不会消失，它们会被新一代产品取代。
