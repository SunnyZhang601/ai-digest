**AI & I by Every — How Sam Altman Uses Dots to Take Back His Time**

The Takeaway: Sam Altman believes AI's real promise is not replacing people but giving them back control of their time and attention, and his own daily use of OpenAI's new "Dots" product is his proof of concept.

Sam Altman, CEO of OpenAI, argues that the coming wave of AI is a renaissance rather than an industrial revolution, and the distinction matters. In a renaissance, humans stay at the center, wielding new tools to expand what they can create. In an industrial revolution, people become cogs in a machine, specialized and disconnected from what makes life good. Altman is betting hard on the first outcome, and he is unusually candid about why: people simply do not care what the machines do, they care about the people behind the art, the company, the idea.

The most concrete example is his own mornings. Altman used to guard early mornings for deep, creative work, but running a large company meant overnight disasters that got worse the longer he ignored them. Checking his phone immediately solved that but polluted his brain and killed his deep work. His Dot now acts as a filter: it exercises judgment about what he actually needs to see, handles the rest, and if something is truly urgent, flags it. The result, he says, is getting his creative space and time with his kids back.

He tells a striking story about a Slack search he gave up on after 10 to 20 minutes, assuming he had dreamed it. The next day his Dot told him it had kept thinking about it overnight and found it, a screenshot rather than text, located by scanning image-heavy screenshots. "It just sat there very patiently looking for something to do to be helpful to me."

On speed, Altman admits he does all his prompting on Ultrafast, and that the difference to his iterative thinking loop is wild. He cites a Brett Victor talk about creators needing an immediate connection to what they are creating. He also frames the pricing philosophy cleanly: OpenAI has focused on bringing down the price of IQ, and now speed becomes a new axis to democratize, with 8X at a great price soon and 100X faster later, while a real frontier always exists at a higher price.

核心要点：Sam Altman 相信 AI 真正的承诺不是取代人，而是把时间和注意力还给人们，而他自己每天使用 OpenAI 新产品 Dots 就是最好的证明。

OpenAI CEO Sam Altman 认为，即将到来的 AI 浪潮更像是一场文艺复兴，而不是工业革命，这个区别很重要。在文艺复兴中，人始终处于中心，用新工具扩展自己能创造的东西。在工业革命中，人变成了机器里的齿轮，被专门化，与让生活美好的那些部分脱节。Altman 坚定押注第一种结果，而且他异常坦率地说明了原因：人们根本不在乎机器做了什么，他们在乎的是艺术、公司、想法背后的人。

最具体的例子就是他自己的早晨。Altman 过去会把清晨留给深度、创造性的工作，但经营一家大公司意味着夜里总会出状况，而且拖得越久越糟。立刻看手机会解决这个问题，却会污染他的大脑，毁掉深度工作时段。现在他的 Dot 充当过滤器：它判断他真正需要看什么，处理其余的事，如果真有紧急情况就标出来。他说，结果是重新拿回了创作空间和陪孩子的时间。

他讲了一个很打动人的故事：有一次他花了一二十分钟在 Slack 里搜索某样东西，最后放弃了，以为自己是在做梦。第二天，他的 Dot 告诉他，它一整晚都在想这件事，并帮他找到了，那其实是一张截图而不是文字，是通过扫描大量图片截图找到的。"它就非常耐心地待在那里，寻找能帮上我的事情做。"

关于速度，Altman 承认自己所有的 prompt 都跑在 Ultrafast 上，而这对他的迭代思考循环带来的差异是巨大的。他引用了 Brett Victor 的一场演讲，讲的是创作者需要与自己正在创造的东西保持即时的连接。他也清晰地阐述了定价哲学：OpenAI 一直专注于降低"智商的价格"，现在速度成为需要民主化的新维度，很快会以很好的价格提供 8 倍速度，之后还有 100 倍更快，而真正的前沿永远存在于更高的价格上。
