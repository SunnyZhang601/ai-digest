**Latent Space: The Revolution vs The Renaissance — Thariq Shihipar, OpenAI**

The Takeaway: The bottleneck in AI-assisted building is no longer intelligence or tokens, it is the experience of working with the model, and the fix is putting the human feedback loop back into the middle of the process instead of optimizing it away.

Thariq Shihipar, most recently of Anthropic and newly announced at OpenAI, argues that AI is simultaneously an industrial revolution and a renaissance, and that most teams have over-indexed on the revolution. The industrial revolution side is about efficiency, reliability, and a standard level of quality, but it also produces a lot of slop. The renaissance side is about making it affordable to build something personal, to raise the level of your art, and to spend human-in-the-loop time refining how something feels. Both can happen in the same project: he plans to write his game engine in WebAssembly for performance while keeping a light scripting layer on top for play.

His most counterintuitive claim is about where he is actually constrained. "I don't even really feel like I'm bottlenecked by intelligence maybe. Like, maybe I'm bottlenecked by ambition." He is not limited by tokens on his $200 plans, and he thinks the real gap is ways of working with the model. Vibe coding disorients him because the model makes reasonable assumptions he never sees, so he built a `/human` folder with its own linting rules, high comment density, no single-character variable names, and files that read like specs but are real code he can play with.

He also pushes back on the idea that models only know how to do things one way. He was "Bash is all you need pilled" and thought React would be the ultimate language because that is what models write, but as models get more general they work in whatever format you want, including drawing a frontend inside Figma. On decision models, he thinks harnesses under-utilize them: a support bot is basically a tree of things happening, and a decision model can handle the routing while opting into inference when it needs a tool call. On game design he is specific: "A perfectly balanced game is boring," and when he had Claude run thousands of simulated games of his card game Frontier Lab, it found that economic victory always wins, and Claude was really bad at fixing the imbalance.

Thariq Shihipar（最近在 Anthropic，刚宣布加入 OpenAI）认为 AI 同时是一场工业革命和一场文艺复兴，而大多数团队过度押注在"革命"这一侧。工业革命那一侧讲的是效率、可靠性和标准化的质量水平，但它也会产出大量 slop。文艺复兴那一侧讲的是让"做点个人的东西"变得可负担、提升你作品的艺术水准、花人在回路里的时间去打磨"感觉"。两者可以发生在同一个项目里：他打算用 WebAssembly 写游戏引擎保证性能，同时在上面保留一层轻量脚本层用来玩。

他最反直觉的观点是关于自己真正的瓶颈在哪。"我甚至不太觉得自己是被智能卡住了。也许我是被野心卡住了。"他在 200 美元套餐上并没有被 token 限制，他认为真正的缺口是和模型协作的方式。Vibe coding 让他很迷失，因为模型会做一些他从没看见的合理假设，所以他建了一个 `/human` 文件夹，有自己的 lint 规则、高密度注释、不用单字符变量名，文件读起来像 spec，但其实是能让他上手玩的真实代码。

他也反驳了"模型只会用一种方式做事"的想法。他曾经是"Bash 就够了"的信徒，也以为 React 会是终极语言，因为模型只会写 React，但模型越通用，就越能用你想要的任何格式工作，包括在 Figma 里画前端。关于 decision model，他认为现在的 harness 用得还不够：一个客服 bot 本质上就是一棵事件树，decision model 可以做路由，需要工具调用时再切到推理。在游戏设计上他说得很具体："一个完全平衡的游戏是无聊的"，当他让 Claude 对自己的卡牌游戏 Frontier Lab 跑了上千局模拟，发现经济胜利总是赢，而 Claude 在修复这种不平衡上表现很差。
