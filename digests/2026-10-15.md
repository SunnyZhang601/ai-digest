**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — "Jev: The AI That Makes Decisions, Not Text - and Why It Changes Everything (Diogo Almeida)"**

The Takeaway: The next wave of AI value is not smarter chat, it is machine-native intelligence that returns probabilities instead of prose, so software can finally branch on judgment the way it has always branched on bits.

Diogo Almeida is CEO of TypeSafe, the company behind Jev, and a co-inventor of RLHF from his years at OpenAI. TypeSafe spent two years in stealth and launched Jev a month ago, and the reaction was immediate: millions of users, a trillion tokens a day, and a massive Series A. Almeida's core argument is that AI has a "gigantic overpromise under deliver issue," and that the field's obsession with chat is the reason. Language models are trained to be pleasing, which makes them great at sounding right and unreliable at being right. He calls this "95% Einstein, 5% Mr. Bean," and points out the fatal flaw: "And if it doesn't tell you when it's the Mr. Bean, then how are you gonna automate anything?"

His fix is a different shape of model. Jev does not generate text, it classifies, outputting probabilities that developers can threshold and control. Almeida insists this is not a downgrade. "Classification is AI complete," he argues, because a perfect general classifier could be turned into anything, including text. The real unlock is composability: instead of writing a giant system prompt full of natural language if-statements that silently fail, you build small reliable classifiers ("smart if statements") that can be measured, evaluated, and stacked into bigger units of automation. He notes that Facebook, TikTok, and Google all run on classifiers, and that being called a classifier is a compliment.

He is sharply critical of the industry's training paradigms. RLHF optimized models to please humans, producing sycophancy, over-refusal, and mode dropping. RLVR crushes benchmarks by construction but has not changed real-world automation. His own approach, RLCD, optimizes for machine-native intelligence, and it required inventing an entirely new shape of training data built in-house. On the wave of copycats that appeared within three weeks of launch, he is blunt: matching the interface is easy, and he suspects competitors are years behind on reliability. He also warns that benchmarks mislead most the users they claim to help, the ones with no time to test tools themselves.

**The MAD Podcast with Matt Turck — "Jev: The AI That Makes Decisions, Not Text - and Why It Changes Everything (Diogo Almeida)"**

The Takeaway：下一波 AI 的价值不在于更聪明的聊天，而在于 machine-native intelligence：它返回的是概率，而不是文字，这样软件终于能像过去对 bit 做分支一样，对判断做分支。

Diogo Almeida 是 TypeSafe 的 CEO，公司产品是 Jev，他本人也是 RLHF 的共同发明人之一，曾在 OpenAI 工作。TypeSafe 隐身两年，一个月前发布 Jev，反响立刻爆发：数百万用户、每天一万亿 token，以及一笔巨额 A 轮融资。Almeida 的核心论点是：AI 存在 "gigantic overpromise under deliver issue"（巨大的过度承诺、交付不足问题），而整个行业对 chat 的执念正是原因所在。语言模型被训练成讨人喜欢，这让它们很擅长"听起来对"，却不可靠地"真的对"。他称之为 "95% Einstein, 5% Mr. Bean"，并指出致命缺陷："And if it doesn't tell you when it's the Mr. Bean, then how are you gonna automate anything?"（如果它不告诉你哪 5% 是憨豆先生，那你还怎么自动化任何事？）

他的解法是一种不同形态的模型。Jev 不生成文字，而是做分类，输出概率，让开发者自己设阈值、做控制。Almeida 坚持这不是降级。他认为 "Classification is AI complete"（分类是 AI 完备的），因为一个完美的通用分类器可以被转化成任何东西，包括文字。真正的解锁点是 composability：与其写一个塞满自然语言 if 语句、还会悄悄出错的巨型 system prompt，不如构建一个个小而可靠的分类器（"smart if statements"），它们可以被度量、被 eval、被堆叠成更大的自动化单元。他指出 Facebook、TikTok、Google 全都跑在分类器上，被人叫作分类器其实是一种赞美。

他对行业的训练范式批评得很直接。RLHF 把模型优化成讨人类喜欢，带来了 sycophancy、过度拒答和 mode dropping。RLVR 在 benchmark 上按构造就能碾压，但并没有改变真实世界的自动化水平。他自己的方法 RLCD 针对 machine-native intelligence 做优化，为此不得不发明一种全新形态的训练数据，并且全部自建。对于发布三周内涌现的一批模仿者，他毫不客气：抄界面很容易，他怀疑竞争对手在可靠性上落后好几年。他还警告，benchmark 最容易误导的恰恰是它声称要帮助的人：那些没时间亲自试用工具的用户。
