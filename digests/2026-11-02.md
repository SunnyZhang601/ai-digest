## PODCASTS

**Latent Space — Building Legal Agents When You Can't See the Data - Jacob Lauritzen, CTO of Legora**

The Takeaway: In regulated industries, the hardest engineering problem isn't model quality, it's building a great agent when you are contractually forbidden from ever looking at your users' data.

Legora CTO Jacob Lauridsen (the Danish name means "son of Laurits") has grown the Stockholm-founded legal AI company from a ChatGPT wrapper over Swedish law into a 100-engineer operation serving Big Law and in-house teams, and his framing of the business is refreshingly unsentimental. The company started as legal research, then expanded into doing the work (drafting, editing Word files, running complex analyses, and its Tabular Review feature, which bulk-extracts answers across up to a million documents in a single table) and orchestrating the work (Lists, Intake, Portal).

His most counterintuitive claim is that better models keep solving problems for free. A citation bug that made outputs hard to follow went from roughly 70% to 99% accuracy "with the next model release" — "It's just like we did nothing and, like, great." That's why evals were deprioritized for a long time: "there was so much to be gained from building a better harness." Now the evals team is three engineers plus three lawyers, backed by a pool of roughly 100 "legal engineers" — Big Law lawyers with CS degrees — who dogfood the product daily.

The genuinely hard constraint is privacy. Legora can't inspect any traces because the data belongs to clients, not the law firms. So the team approximates real usage with synthetic eval sets, classifies failures into known types, and stays deliberately conservative: "we restrict ourselves as most possible." Everything runs on Azure because early on law firms trusted Microsoft (already an approved vendor via 365) but not AWS or Google. Model choice is arbitrage across the Pareto frontier — frontier models where needed, open-weight models post-trained for specific tasks, and a surprising shout-out to an early Gemini for transcription and speaker attribution.

On the profession, he's blunt that the pyramid gets repriced rather than destroyed: "Paralegals, gone... junior lawyers, gone" is the joke, but the reality is that good junior lawyers become worth far more, and the partnership model's real problem is that partners have no incentive to codify expertise into durable firm IP they don't own.

Jacob Lauridsen（这个丹麦姓氏的意思是"Laurits 之子"）是 Legora 的 CTO。这家在斯德哥尔摩创立的法律 AI 公司，从"ChatGPT 加瑞典法律检索"起步，如今已是 100 名工程师的规模，服务大型律所和企业内部法务团队。他对这门生意的描述非常不煽情：先是做法律检索，然后扩展到"做工作本身"（起草、编辑 Word 文件、跑复杂分析，以及 Tabular Review 这个能在单张表里对多达一百万份文档批量抽取答案的功能），再到"编排工作"（Lists、Intake、Portal）。

他最反直觉的观点是：更好的模型一直在免费帮他解决问题。曾经有个引用不准、导致结果难以追溯的 bug，在"下一次模型发布"后准确率从大约 70% 直接跳到 99%。"就像我们什么都没做，然后就，太好了。"这也是他们长期不重视 evals 的原因："把 harness 做好能带来的收益太多了。"现在 evals 团队是三名工程师加三名律师，背后还有大约 100 名"legal engineer"（有 CS 学位、在 Big Law 干过三四年的律师）每天用自家产品。

真正硬的约束是隐私。Legora 看不到任何 trace，因为数据属于客户，不属于律所。所以团队只能用合成的 eval 集去逼近真实使用场景，把失败归入已知类型，并且刻意保守："我们尽可能把自己限制到最严。"所有基础设施都跑在 Azure 上，因为早期律所信任微软（365 已经是获批供应商），但不信任 AWS 或 Google。模型选择则是在帕累托前沿上做套利：需要时用前沿模型，特定任务用后训练过的开源权重模型，还特别点名了早期某个 Gemini 在转写和说话人识别上出奇地好。

关于这个行业，他很直白：金字塔是被重新定价，而不是被摧毁。"Paralegal，没了……初级律师，没了"是玩笑，但现实是优秀的初级律师变得值钱得多；而合伙制真正的问题是，合伙人没有动力把专业知识沉淀成公司拥有、而他们自己带不走的持久 IP。
