**Notion's Founder on the One Thing Your AI Is Missing** (AI & I by Every)

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=PGWvMrF4XIA

The Takeaway: AI agents can now generate any workflow or interface, so the durable asset is no longer software logic or UI but the structured context and data a team owns, and whoever keeps that context safe and portable wins.

Ivan Zhao, co-founder and CEO of Notion, has spent 13 years chasing an idea borrowed from the 1960s and 70s: that software should be a medium everyone can read and write, like prose, rather than something frozen into applications by a professional class of developers. Notion was his attempt to bring that computing power to people without forcing them to learn to code, and he sees the current coding-agent wave as that original vision finally arriving.

What makes his take worth listening to is that he refuses to treat "anyone can build anything" as an unqualified good. He splits the world into three buckets: tools you should absolutely be free to modify yourself, everyday objects you want an expert to craft for you (he points to Notion's newly rebuilt native calendar app), and infrastructure like enterprise security that you should let someone else maintain. His line on the second bucket is memorable: "Yes, you can cook yourself, but oftentimes you would like to go to a restaurant as well."

His sharper point is about what AI makes obsolete. If agents can write the procedural code and generate the UI, then the workflow logic and the buttons are no longer the moat, the data and semantics are. He describes Notion's job as increasingly being "the keeper of your data," opening it to outside agents while keeping human teams collaborating on it. And he flags a second-order effect he worries about: he now prefers chatting with a model to reading on his Kindle, and sometimes it feels like TikTok. His proposed antidote is understanding, arguing we should not surrender our grasp of what these systems do without a fight, and that tool makers should build better abstractions and visualizations so people can keep up.

Ivan Zhao，Notion 的联合创始人兼 CEO，花了 13 年追逐一个源自 1960 和 70 年代的想法：软件应该是一种人人都能读写的媒介，像散文一样，而不是被专业开发者冻结在应用里。Notion 就是他试图在不强迫人们学编程的前提下把这种计算能力带给更多人的尝试，而他认为当前的 coding agent 浪潮正是那个最初愿景终于到来。

他的观点值得一听的地方在于，他拒绝把 "人人都能构建任何东西" 当作无条件的好事。他把世界分成三类：你绝对应该有权自己修改的工具；你希望由专家为你精心打造、日常使用的东西（他以 Notion 刚重建的原生日历 app 为例）；以及像企业安全这样应该交给别人维护的基础设施。他对第二类的说法令人印象深刻："是的，你可以自己做饭，但很多时候你也想去餐厅。"

他更犀利的一点是关于 AI 让什么变得不再重要。如果 agent 能写流程代码并生成 UI，那么工作流逻辑和按钮就不再是护城河，数据和语义才是。他把 Notion 的职责描述为越来越像是 "你数据的守护者"，把数据开放给外部 agent，同时让人类团队继续在其上协作。他还指出一个让他担忧的二阶效应：他现在更喜欢和模型聊天而不是在 Kindle 上阅读，有时感觉就像在刷 TikTok。他提出的解药是理解，认为我们不应该不加抵抗地放弃对这些系统在做什么的把握，工具制作者应该构建更好的抽象和可视化，让人们跟得上。
