**Redesigning source control at Anthropic**

Anthropic Engineering detailed how coding is no longer the bottleneck at Anthropic; developer infrastructure built for human scale now is. The company averaged around 2,500 code changes merged per day in May 2026, and over 30,000 per day by September. To handle this, they built Scion, an in-house version control system, and fully migrated their monorepo from git in five months, alongside a code review surface called Antique, code search, a rich IAM system, and novel ways of landing large amounts of reviewed code. Their main branch history alone is almost 100 GB, with a compressed working copy over 25 GB, and they perform on the order of 25 million code checkouts per day for CI. The post lays out principles for agent-scale source control: clients are always online, client-server asymmetry, a comprehensive service powering novel applications, horizontal scalability, cheap incremental lock-free commits, and optional sparse-by-design working copies. Scion's unit of work is a "changeset" in a write-ahead log, with derived data structures (file blame, path history, skip lists for the commit graph) computed recursively, allowing new indices to be added without changing the client.

Anthropic Engineering 详细介绍了在 Anthropic，写代码已不再是构建系统或做研究的瓶颈，真正的瓶颈变成了为人类规模设计的开发者基础设施。公司在 2026 年 5 月平均每天合并约 2,500 个代码变更，到 9 月已超过每天 30,000 个。为应对这一增长，他们构建了内部版本控制系统 Scion，并在五个月内把 monorepo 从 git 完全迁移过来，同时还构建了名为 Antique 的代码审查界面、代码搜索、完善的 IAM 系统，以及大规模落地已审查代码的新方法。他们的 main 分支历史单独就接近 100 GB，压缩后的工作副本超过 25 GB，每天为 CI 执行约 2,500 万次代码 checkout。文章阐述了面向 agent 规模的版本控制原则：客户端始终在线、客户端-服务器不对称、用统一服务驱动新应用、水平可扩展、廉价且增量的无锁提交，以及可选且天然稀疏的工作副本。Scion 的工作单元是 write-ahead log 中的 "changeset"，其派生数据结构（file blame、path history、commit graph 的 skip list）递归计算，使得新增索引无需改动客户端。

**Training Data: David Silver and Lasse Espeholt of Ineffable Intelligence: A Superlearner for the Age of Experience**

The Takeaway: The path to superintelligence does not run through human language and internet data at all; it runs through systems that discover knowledge entirely for themselves, and the moment you let go of human priors is the moment learning accelerates by orders of magnitude.

David Silver, whose 2009 PhD applied reinforcement learning to Go and who led the AlphaGo and AlphaZero work at DeepMind, has co-founded Ineffable Intelligence with Lasse Espeholt, the engineer behind some of the foundational work on scaling reinforcement learning on GPUs. Their bet is contrarian: the world already has hundreds of thousands of talented researchers doubling down on LLMs, so they chose the "other lamppost" that nobody is looking under. Ineffable is building a "super learner," a system with both the depth to start from knowing nothing and discover things beyond all human knowledge, and the breadth to be dropped into any stream of experience, simulated or physical. They call the environments where this happens "crucibles," worlds engineered for emergent complexity rather than handcrafted puzzles.

Silver's core argument is that simple objectives in complex environments produce complexity far beyond their starting point, the way evolution's fitness signal produced all of life and the way human coordination led to language, trade, arithmetic, tools, and science in sequence. He is blunt about the LLM path: "We're giving the agent some simple objectives and asking it to discover things that will surprise us and amaze us," rather than "pulling the agent by its nose towards a particular desired outcome." The AlphaGo-to-AlphaZero story is his proof: two years of human-in-the-loop work to beat Lee Sedol, then a few hours to surpass it once they dropped human data. On alignment, he argues language-based monitoring is reaching its limits as agents learn to say one thing and act another, and proposes alternatives like shadow actions and superintelligent monitors. His position on pacing is nuanced: slow down at the real-world frontier, but move as fast as possible in simulation, because that is where the science of superintelligence gets figured out safely.

The Takeaway：通往超级智能的路径根本不经过人类语言和互联网数据；它走的是完全靠系统自己去发现知识的路线，而一旦你放弃人类先验，学习速度就会以数量级提升。

David Silver 的 2009 年博士论文把强化学习应用到围棋上，并在 DeepMind 主导了 AlphaGo 和 AlphaZero 的工作；他与 Lasse Espeholt 共同创立了 Ineffable Intelligence，后者是 GPU 上强化学习规模化的一些基础性工作的作者。他们的赌注是反共识的：世界上已经有几十万聪明的研究者在死磕 LLM，所以他们选择了那盏"没人去看的路灯"。Ineffable 要构建的是一个 "super learner"，它既有深度，能从一无所知出发、发现超越全部人类知识的东西；也有广度，能被丢进任何经验流中，无论是模拟世界还是物理世界。他们把这种系统所处环境称为 "crucible"，即为了涌现复杂性而设计的世界，而不是手工拼凑的谜题集。

Silver 的核心论点是：复杂环境中的简单目标，会产生远超起点的复杂性，就像进化的适应度信号催生了全部生命，也像人类协作依次催生了语言、贸易、算术、工具和科学。他对 LLM 路线说得很直接："我们给 agent 一些简单的目标，让它去发现那些会让我们惊讶和惊叹的东西"，而不是"牵着 agent 的鼻子走向某个预设结果"。AlphaGo 到 AlphaZero 的故事就是他的证据：花两年时间、人在回路中打磨才击败李世石，而一旦放弃人类数据，只需几个小时就超越了它。在 alignment 上，他认为随着 agent 学会说一套做一套，基于语言的监控正接近极限，并提出 shadow action 和超级智能监控者等替代方案。他对"减速"的立场很微妙：在真实世界的前沿上放慢，但在模拟中尽可能快，因为那里才是安全地搞清超级智能科学的地方。
