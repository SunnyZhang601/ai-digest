**AI & I by Every — "Can You Use AI To Make Good Art? — With Fred Graver"**
The Takeaway: AI is a virtual writer's room, not a ghostwriter. The people who get the most out of it are the ones who first empty out what only they know, then use the model to pressure-test, structure and reach an audience.

Fred Graver has been a Disney Imagineer, a writer for Late Night with David Letterman and Norman Lear, a comedy writer on Cheers and In Living Color, head of TV at Twitter in its second year, and a conversation designer at Microsoft scripting bots with the AI teams. That career is a repeated bet on getting closer to the audience, and he is making the same bet again now, rebuilding a production process from scratch around AI. His framing of the moment is blunt: his old tribe is not thrilled, but there is a new group of storytellers defined by sensibility rather than age who are already playing with these tools.

His method is deliberately low-tech first. Before touching a model, spend a day or two dumping everything you know about the idea onto a page, by hand or by voice, because "you will never have that opportunity again once you open AI." Then bring in the machine as a room of intelligences: a "who I am as a writer" document, a document about the current project, an audience focus group with one person you wish you hadn't invited, and experts whose published craft has been ingested.

He breaks the work into a loop of three phases: ideation and architecture (the script), production (prep, storyboards, editing), and marketing and distribution. Making it a circle means marketing talks to writers before decisions get locked in, which also removes the personal friction that poisons so much of show business. He is specific about what AI cannot do: "If you ask it to tell you what it is you should be saying, you'll get... it goes to the mean." The quirks that make a character endearing, the improvised "D'oh!", are the human residue. His thesis is a show business truism: success is knowing what the audience wants before the audience knows it. AI can supply the audience data and the structure, but the third phase, actually reaching an audience without the golden age of television behind you, is the unsolved part, and he keeps looking for the Kevin Kelly thousand-true-fans equivalent for people who make shows.

**AI & I by Every — "Can You Use AI To Make Good Art? — With Fred Graver"**
核心要点：AI 是一个虚拟编剧室，而不是代笔。最能从中获益的人，是那些先把自己独有的东西倒空，再用模型去压力测试、组织结构、触达观众的人。

Fred Graver 当过 Disney Imagineer，给 Letterman 的 Late Night 和 Norman Lear 写过稿，在 Cheers 和 In Living Color 做过喜剧编剧，在 Twitter 成立第二年担任电视业务负责人，还在 Microsoft 与 AI 团队一起做对话设计、写 bot 脚本。他的职业生涯就是一次次押注"离观众更近"，现在他又押了一次，围绕 AI 从零重建制作流程。他对当下形势的判断很直白：他的老圈子并不兴奋，但有一批新的讲故事的人，由审美和心态而非年龄来定义，他们已经在玩这些工具了。

他的方法刻意从低科技开始。在碰模型之前，先花一两天把关于这个创意的所有东西倒到纸上，手写或口述都行，因为"一旦你打开 AI，你就再也没有这个机会了"。然后把机器当作一个由多种智能组成的房间请进来：一份"作为写作者我是谁"的文档、一份关于当前项目的文档、一个观众焦点小组（里面要有那个你后悔请来的毒舌），以及那些作品已被模型吸收的行业专家。

他把工作拆成一个三阶段循环：构思与架构（剧本）、制作（筹备、分镜、剪辑）、营销与发行。把它做成一个圆，意味着营销在决策锁死之前就能和编剧对话，这也顺带消解了演艺圈里那么多伤人的个人摩擦。他对 AI 做不到什么说得很具体："如果你让它告诉你该说什么，你得到的会是……它会走向平均值。"让角色可爱的那些小怪癖，那句即兴的 "D'oh!"，才是人类的残留。他的论点是演艺圈的老真理：成功就是在观众知道自己想要什么之前就知道他们想要什么。AI 能提供观众数据和结构，但第三阶段，在没有电视黄金时代托底的情况下真正触达观众，仍未解决，他一直在为做节目的人寻找 Kevin Kelly "一千个真爱粉"的等价物。
