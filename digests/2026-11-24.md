**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 98: China Expert on Jensen's Return**
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gkd5eOQGASU

The Takeaway: Jensen Huang's return to NVIDIA is less a vindication than a deal, and the export-control fight over chips is far from over.

Jordan Schneider has run ChinaTalk for a decade, and by his own count this is his fifth emergency episode in under two weeks, behind only the nine he did during COVID. His read on the Jensen saga is that the board could only feel comfortable reinstating him because Trump must have promised some combination of a settlement and pardons over NVIDIA chips reaching China, including a segment of Alibaba doing military research. The fine, he says, is around $25 million, and Democrats plus half the Republican party will be screaming about it for years.

His core charge is not the affair with a Chinese national who was also a customer, though he notes the Chinese government would have had "extremely intimate knowledge" of NVIDIA's business as a result. It is the willful blindness to smuggling. He points to Super Micro executives using hair dryers to peel labels off server racks, and to Jensen going on television for years insisting smuggling was impossible because the racks are too heavy. The analogy he likes is banks and sanctions compliance: for a while Standard Chartered, HSBC and Wells Fargo didn't care either, until Treasury fined them into taking it seriously. NVIDIA, by contrast, designed chips to sell at 99 when the rule said 100.

On the policy side, a 132-page rule landed after the midterms, which he reads as Rubio waiting out an embarrassing story for Republicans. It restricts advanced chip fabrication at TSMC without Commerce approval and cuts off remote access for neo-clouds in Malaysia serving Chinese customers after 90 days. Roughly two fifths of Chinese compute came from Malaysian data centers, so rescinding or not enforcing this rule matters enormously.

His verdict on the "they'd get the chips anyway" argument: Huawei's constraint is supply, not demand, because high bandwidth memory and semiconductor manufacturing equipment are cut off. Keep that up and the West holds a 10 to 15x compute advantage even on a five to seven year horizon. He is also blunt about robotics: at CORAL he watched a DeepMind robot barely pick up a muffin, and every vendor dodged questions about magnets, 99.5% of which come from one place. "It's so embarrassing watching them not work in person."

The quote that captures his view of the whole affair: "I don't see how the genie gets back in the bottle."

Unsupervised Learning — 第 98 期：中国问题专家谈 Jensen 回归
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=gkd5eOQGASU

核心要点：Jensen Huang 回归 NVIDIA 与其说是"平反"，不如说是一笔交易，而围绕芯片出口管制的博弈远未结束。

Jordan Schneider 做 ChinaTalk 已经十年，按他自己的统计，这是不到两周内的第五期紧急节目，仅次于新冠期间的九期。他对 Jensen 事件的判断是：董事会之所以敢让他回归，一定是因为 Trump 承诺了某种组合，包括和解协议以及对 NVIDIA 芯片流入中国的赦免，其中还包括阿里巴巴某个做军事研究的部门。他说罚款大约是 2500 万美元，而民主党加上半个共和党会为此骂上好几年。

他最核心的指控不是那段婚外情（对方是中国人，同时也是客户），尽管他指出这会让中国政府"极其深入地了解"NVIDIA 的业务。他真正在意的是对走私的睁一只眼闭一只眼。他提到 Super Micro 的高管用吹风机把服务器机架上的标签撕下来，也提到 Jensen 多年来在电视上坚称走私不可能，因为机架太重了。他喜欢的类比是银行与制裁合规：当年渣打、汇丰、富国也不在乎，直到财政部罚到他们认真对待。而 NVIDIA 的做法是，规则说不能卖 100，那就设计一款卖 99 的芯片。

政策层面，中期选举后落地了一份 132 页的规则，他解读为 Rubio 在等一个对共和党来说太丢人的故事过去。这份规则要求台积电代工先进芯片需商务部批准，并在 90 天后切断马来西亚 neo-cloud 对中国客户的远程访问。中国大约五分之二的计算力来自马来西亚的数据中心，所以这份规则是被撤销还是不被执行，影响巨大。

对于"反正他们也能拿到芯片"这个论点，他的回应是：华为的瓶颈是供给而不是需求，因为高带宽内存和半导体设备都被卡住了。只要维持住，西方在五到七年的时间尺度上仍能保持 10 到 15 倍的计算力优势。他对机器人也很直白：在 CORAL 大会上，他看着 DeepMind 的机器人连一个松饼都勉强拿起来，而每家厂商都回避关于磁铁的问题，而 99.5% 的磁铁来自同一个地方。"现场看着它们不工作是真的很尴尬。"

最能概括他整体看法的一句话是："我看不出这个精灵怎么还能被塞回瓶子里。"
