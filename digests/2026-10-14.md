**AI & I by Every — How Hugging Face's Investor Spots Winners**

The Takeaway: The single most valuable skill in an AI-native company is no longer coding, it's the ability to generate product insights from real user behavior and iterate on them faster than anyone else.

Matt Hartman is the founder and managing partner of Factorial Capital and, while a partner at Betaworks, wrote the first check into a tiny chatbot company called Hugging Face. He thinks about investing as hunting for "the newly technically possible," a hunt that starts with tinkering, reading papers, and asking smart people questions until a thesis emerges that is "20 degrees off" from where you started. His Hugging Face bet came from tracking the word2vec paper, realizing words could be embedded as vectors of meaning, and finding a team obsessed with the right question: what could you build now that this exists?

Hartman's most counterintuitive claim is that the founder's actual idea matters less than their capacity to pivot. He assumes every early company will pivot, and what he's really underwriting is the "pivot foot," whether a team can move toward something new without traveling. Technical founders get more latitude because their pivots are driven by technology rather than a single vertical's market understanding. He also prizes "earned insights," asking how many people were actually in a position to learn the thing a founder learned.

On where value is going, Hartman argues that if coding gets faster, iteration becomes the whole game. "The ability to synthesize how a user is using your product and then to improve it, I think, has got to be exponentially more value." He points to companies like Modal and Factory growing far faster than Hugging Face's ten-year climb, crediting compressed build-and-iterate cycles. His current themes: developer tools (including maintenance tooling for all this AI-generated code), frontier non-language models like robotics and science, and a new kind of full-stack application company that can go all the way down the stack, the way early Instagram shaved load time through a design decision.

Matt Hartman 是 Factorial Capital 的创始人兼管理合伙人，当年在 Betaworks 做合伙人时，给一家名叫 Hugging Face 的小聊天机器人公司开出了第一张支票。他把投资看作寻找"新近变得技术上可行"的东西，这个过程始于折腾、读论文、向聪明人提问，直到浮现出一个与你出发点"偏了 20 度"的 thesis。他对 Hugging Face 的押注，来自追踪 word2vec 论文、意识到词可以被嵌入为承载意义的向量，并找到一个痴迷于正确问题的团队：既然这东西存在了，你能做出什么？

Hartman 最反直觉的观点是，创始人的具体想法不如其转向能力重要。他假设每家早期公司都会转型，而他真正在押注的是"中枢脚"（pivot foot），即团队能否在移动的同时不"走步"。技术型创始人拥有更大自由度，因为他们的转向由技术驱动，而非对某个垂直市场的理解。他也看重"挣来的洞察"（earned insights），会问究竟有多少人真正处在能学到创始人那个发现的位置上。

谈到价值流向，Hartman 认为如果写代码变快，迭代就成了全部。他说："把用户如何使用你的产品综合起来、然后改进它的能力，我认为其价值一定是呈指数级上升的。"他指出像 Modal 和 Factory 这样的公司，增长速度远超 Hugging Face 花十年才走完的历程，靠的正是被压缩的"构建-迭代"循环。他当前关注的主题包括：开发者工具（包括为这些 AI 生成代码做维护的工具）、机器人和科学等前沿非语言模型，以及一种新型全栈应用公司，能一路打通整条技术栈，就像早期 Instagram 通过一个设计决策削减了加载时间。
