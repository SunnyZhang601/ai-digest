**Box CEO Aaron Levie**

**AI & I by Every: Why Every Traded Personal Agents for One Company Agent**

The Takeaway: For work, one shared company agent beats a company full of personal agents, while personal agents will win at home, and the dividing line between the two is hardening fast.

Dan Shipper, CEO of Every, and Willie Williams, Every's head of platform, trace a full cycle: the company went all in on personal agents with Open Claw, built a platform for everyone to spin up their own, watched those agents die off, and then built a single company agent that the whole team now depends on. The core argument is about who wants to manage complexity. Builders love knobs and tinkering, operators just want something that works, and most employees stopped maintaining their agents the moment a better option appeared. Williams puts it plainly: "we want, instead of everyone investing in their own agents and making them better day to day over time, it's like, why don't we take all that energy and put it into one agent that the whole company benefits from?" A single agent also accumulates institutional context that a newcomer agent can't replicate, and naming and remembering which agent had which expertise became its own tax.

Shipper bets that in a year he'll still use a persistent personal agent as a portal to his own command center, but it won't be the cute named character he has now. Williams expects a split: a company agent at work where everyone contributes to the same system, and a personalized agent at home for family life. On engineering management, Williams says the job feels the most unchanged of any role he's held, still fundamentally about people and removing bottlenecks. What changed is leverage: dashboards, anomaly detection, and first-line on-call can now be handled by agents, freeing a manager's energy for the conversations that still require a human. He also notes his own token usage fell off a cliff when he moved back into management, because there's no billion-token move that replaces sitting down with someone.
