**Vercel CEO Guillermo Rauch**

Guillermo Rauch argued that the #0 skill in the AI era is managing how much you guide the model: over-guide it and you waste a system that often knows better than you, under-guide it and it drifts off-track with the wrong biases or missing context. Separately, he noticed he loves the engineering vernacular models use, words like gate, evidence, culprit, durable, provenance and contract, and traced it to most engineering being debugging and forensics. That led him to wonder what the "minimum viable English" for productivity actually is, noting that ASD-STE100 Simplified Technical English has about 900 approved words and suspecting we need far fewer.

Guillermo Rauch 认为，AI 时代的第一技能是管理你对模型"指导多少"：指导过头，就浪费了一个往往比你更懂的系统；指导不足，它又会跑偏、带上错误偏见或漏掉上下文。另外他发现自己很喜欢模型使用的工程词汇，比如 gate、evidence、culprit、durable、provenance、contract，并认为这源于大部分工程工作其实是在 debug 和理解已有系统，也就是做取证。由此他好奇，为了高效工作，"最小可用英语"到底是什么，并提到 ASD-STE100 简化技术英语大约有 900 个核准词，而他猜我们需要的词会少得多。

**Box CEO Aaron Levie**

Aaron Levie predicted that soon there will be 1,000X more tokens flowing through cyber agents than today, as the surface area of cyber risk grows beyond what any company can track manually. He described agent swarms that fan out, use any security tool, test attack variations, read logs and score enterprise data for risk, and stressed this only became possible in the past few months now that long-running agents, computer use and cyber-trained models are good enough. He called it a huge opportunity across every layer of the security stack, from identity and firewalls to code scanning and data protection, and a reason to be optimistic about the offense-defense balance.

Aaron Levie 预测，很快流经网络安全 agent 的 token 量会是今天的 1000 倍，因为网络风险的暴露面已经增长到任何公司都难以人工追踪的程度。他描述了能铺开作业的 agent 集群：调用各类安全工具、测试各种攻击变体、通读日志、对企业数据做风险评级。他强调这只是过去几个月才成为可能，前提是长时运行的 agent、computer use 和经过深度网络安全训练的模型都足够好了。他说这是安全栈每一层的巨大机会，从身份、防火墙到代码扫描和数据保护，也是他对攻防平衡保持乐观的一个理由。

**Cursor designer Ryo Lu**

Ryo Lu wrote a long reflection arguing that struggle is where real skill and real relationships come from, and worrying that AI removes the difficulty before we understand what it was teaching us. He traced how he learned programming and design through repeated failure until it "became like water," and noted that with a machine you can get a result that looks complete while never learning the questions you should have asked. His bigger concern is social: agents are always patient and available, so it becomes easier than reaching toward a person who might misunderstand you or disagree, and he pictures everyone in their own little agent-shaped world. He ended on wanting tools that push us toward the world and other people, like a jazz band where nobody knows where it will end up, rather than "the silent static of the server farm."

Ryo Lu 写了一篇长文反思，认为真正的技能和真正的关系都来自挣扎，并担心 AI 在我们还没理解困难教会了我们什么之前就把它拿走了。他回顾自己是怎么在反复失败中学会编程和设计的，直到那些东西"像水一样"自然，并指出有了机器之后，你能拿到一个看起来完整的结果，却从未学会本该提出的问题。他更大的担忧在社交层面：agent 永远耐心、永远在线，于是去找一个可能误解你、可能不同意你的人反而更难了，他想象每个人都待在自己那个 agent 形状的小世界里。他最后说，想要的是把人推向世界和他人的工具，像一支爵士乐队，没人知道会走到哪里，而不是"服务器农场里无声的静电"。
