**No Priors — Superintelligence Needs the Physical World with CEO and Co-Founder of Arda Bob McGrew**

The Takeaway: You cannot reach superintelligence by automating researchers alone, because the bottleneck is not thinking but atoms, and the physical world is where the next set of S-curves has to be climbed.

Bob McGrew was an early executive at Palantir, then chief research officer at OpenAI, and is now CEO and co-founder of Arda, which builds software and AI for manufacturing engineering. His argument is refreshingly contrarian in a moment obsessed with recursive self-improvement: the "hard takeoff" story is straightforwardly wrong. Automating a researcher's time does not compound into a singularity, because researchers were never the constraint. "The actual limiting factor was not the number of researchers we could hire. It was the amount of GPUs that we had on hand." Automating research is a big one-time acceleration, like the jump from a model without reasoning to one with reasoning and tools, but it is another S-curve, not a self-improvement loop.

He extends the logic to everything physical. Look outside and almost nothing differs from ten years before AI. Even a Waymo is only halfway into the physical world: the car still does not fly and still sits in the same traffic. Cheap hypotheses make wet labs, factories and permits the scarce complements, and frontier labs have no particular comparative advantage in building those. That is the gap Arda is going after: American manufacturing is great at design, bad at production, because automation is bespoke and cannot generalize from one iPhone generation to the next. Arda wants to make turning over an automated line as easy as retraining a human worker, targeting defense, consumer electronics and the data center supply chain, where designs change constantly and almost everything is still assembled by hand. On robotics, he thinks two arms and a camera, sometimes one arm, is enough, that the hardware already works and the problem is economics, and that frontier models serve best as an orchestrator over specialized tools rather than as the whole brain.
