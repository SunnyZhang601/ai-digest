**Aaron Levie (CEO, Box)**

Aaron Levie made the case that agent swarms will be a very big deal because compute finally becomes a critical variable in a large number of economically important tasks. Much work in the world, from research and data processing to discovery, testing and QA, is bottlenecked by our ability to plan, execute, review and coordinate, so we do very little of it relative to its value. Once agents can use tools, write code, process data, have domain depth and coordinate with each other, the only parameter that matters in the limit is how many tokens you throw at the problem, which reshapes cybersecurity, life sciences, materials research, trading and more.

Aaron Levie 认为 agent swarm 会变得非常重要，因为 compute 终于成为大量经济上重要任务的关键变量。世界上很多工作，从研究、数据处理到发现、测试和 QA，瓶颈都在于我们计划、执行、审查和协调的能力，所以相对其价值而言我们做得非常少。一旦 agent 能用工具、写代码、处理数据、具备领域深度并彼此协作，极限情况下唯一重要的参数就是你往问题上砸多少 token，这会重塑网络安全、生命科学、材料研究、交易等领域。

**Anthropic Engineering**

**How we contain Claude across products**
Anthropic explains why it now routinely grants Claude access that could take down an internal service, a level it would have rejected a year ago. Risk has two parts: the likelihood of failure, which safeguards and training have driven down, and the blast radius, which only grows with capability. The post argues human-in-the-loop approval is fallible, since telemetry showed users approve roughly 93% of permission prompts, so Anthropic leans on containment instead: sandboxes, VMs, filesystem boundaries and egress controls. It breaks agent risk into user misuse, model misbehavior and external attackers, and describes three isolation patterns across claude.ai, Claude Code and Claude Cowork. On Gray Swan's Agent Red Teaming benchmark, Claude Opus 4.7 holds attack success to roughly 0.1% on single attempts and 5 to 6% after 100 adaptive attempts, while Claude Code auto mode catches roughly 83% of overeager behaviors before execution.

**How we contain Claude across products**
Anthropic 解释了为什么现在会常规性地给 Claude 足以搞挂内部服务的权限，而一年前这种权限是会被直接否掉的。风险分两部分：失败的概率，这方面 safeguards 和模型训练已经把它压下来了；以及爆炸半径，这部分只会随能力增长而变大。文章认为 human-in-the-loop 审批并不可靠，因为遥测数据显示用户批准了大约 93% 的权限弹窗，所以 Anthropic 更依赖 containment：sandbox、VM、文件系统边界和 egress 控制。它把 agent 风险分为用户误用、模型不当行为和外部攻击者三类，并描述了 claude.ai、Claude Code 和 Claude Cowork 三种隔离模式。在 Gray Swan 的 Agent Red Teaming benchmark 上，Claude Opus 4.7 单次尝试的攻击成功率约为 0.1%，100 次自适应尝试后为 5 到 6%，而 Claude Code auto mode 能在执行前拦下大约 83% 的过度行为。

**An update on recent Claude Code quality reports**
Anthropic traced complaints of degraded Claude responses to three separate changes affecting Claude Code, the Claude Agent SDK and Claude Cowork, with the API unaffected. First, the default reasoning effort was lowered from high to medium on March 4 to cut latency, then reverted April 7 after users said they preferred higher intelligence by default. Second, a March 26 caching optimization meant to clear stale thinking after an hour of idle time had a bug that cleared thinking every turn for the rest of the session, making Claude seem forgetful and repetitive, and also causing cache misses that drained usage limits faster; it was fixed April 10. Third, an April 16 system prompt instruction capping tool-call text at 25 words and final responses at 100 words hurt coding quality and was reverted April 20. Anthropic is resetting usage limits for all subscribers as of April 23 and tightening controls on system prompt changes.

## PODCASTS

**No Priors — Beam: The Great American Open Model with ReflectionAI Co-Founder and CEO Misha Laskin**

The Takeaway: Open models are no longer a charity project or a fallback. They are becoming the default substrate for enterprise intelligence, and whoever can serve the most intelligence density per unit of compute, with the most trust, wins.

Misha Laskin is co-founder and CEO of Reflection AI, an open-weight lab he started after working on the reinforcement learning team behind the first Gemini models at Google DeepMind. His co-founder Janus led that RL work and was a founding engineer at DeepMind contributing to AlphaGo. Reflection's first model, Beam, is a 500 billion parameter model with 23 billion active parameters, and Laskin argues the company had to build it end to end because the good open models were all coming from China and reinforcement learning only works well at scale if you own the pretraining base.

The most counterintuitive claim is about cost. Frontier pretraining is not the impossible barrier people assume. Laskin pegs catching up at hundreds of millions of dollars a year ago, single-digit billions more recently, and order tens of billions going into next year, with roughly a 4x compute multiplier per model generation. But he thinks the curve has to asymptote, because the intelligence extracted per training FLOP is rising fast. Pretraining efficiency gains run around 7x a year, and RL efficiency gains may be closer to 30x.

On safety, Laskin is sharply contrarian. He invokes Linus's law: with enough eyeballs, most security and safety vulnerabilities become shallow. A few hundred safety researchers inside closed labs cannot cover the long tail of unintended consequences, and he points to a powerful closed model hacking another company where the only remediation was open models. His framing: "open models are Trojan horses for the infrastructure that they bring with them." He also predicts the open versus closed token split, already flipped from 70/30 closed to roughly 70/30 open on gateways, will keep moving toward open, much like Linux dominates servers.
