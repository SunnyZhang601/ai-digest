**Nan Yu (head of product, Codex at OpenAI; formerly head of product at Linear)**

Nan Yu made a sharp product argument: choose the "dumbest" architecture that works, not the "simplest," because simple is something you iterate toward while dumb is the correct starting point. Being clever, he argues, forces your users to be just as clever to understand your cleverness. He also observed that software has gotten much easier if you know what you want to build and much harder if you don't: with clarity you can straight-shot to a solution, without it you ship a pile of junk and spend a lot of money doing it.

Nan Yu 提出了一个犀利的 product 观点：选"最笨"但能跑通的架构，而不是"最简单"的，因为 simple 是你迭代出来的结果，而 dumb 才是正确的起点。他认为，耍聪明意味着用户也必须同样聪明才能理解你的聪明。他还观察到，如果你清楚自己要做什么，软件开发变得容易得多；如果不清楚，就难得多：有清晰度就能直奔解法，没有清晰度就会交付一堆垃圾，还花掉很多钱。

**Guillermo Rauch (CEO, Vercel)**

Guillermo Rauch built a physical simulation of a record player using WebGL2 and Web Audio, and calls it "shockingly" accurate. Rather than playing a song, it converts the audio into the physical shape of a groove, then a simulated stylus rides those walls 384,000 times per second and you hear the voltage that motion would induce in a cartridge. The crackle, pops, wow and flutter, and dull inner grooves are all natural byproducts of the simulation, and you can even DJ the record. The code is linked for remixing.

Guillermo Rauch 用 WebGL2 和 Web Audio 做了一个黑胶唱机的物理模拟，并称其准确得"令人震惊"。它不是播放歌曲，而是把音频转换成唱片纹路的物理形状，然后模拟唱针每秒 384,000 次划过这些沟壁，你听到的是这种运动在唱头中感应出的电压。爆裂声、噼啪声、抖晃和内侧音轨的沉闷感都是模拟的自然副产物，你甚至可以给唱片打碟。代码已链接，可自行 remix。

**Box CEO Aaron Levie**

Aaron Levie argued that AI is shifting from an era where output arrives at roughly the pace you can chat back and forth, to one where you hand a problem to a set of agents that go do an arbitrarily large amount of work, moving productivity gains from 20-50% to gains with no real upper limit. Agent swarms fanning out complex work in parallel will produce qualitatively different outcomes in fields like cybersecurity, life sciences, and any domain where computer-based work can be parallelized. His deeper point: our brains aren't wired for a world where compute is the variable we flex to solve any problem, because we're used to time-in correlating with output-out.

Box CEO Aaron Levie 认为，AI 正从"输出速度大致等于你能来回对话的速度"的时代，转向把问题交给一组 agent 去完成任意规模工作的时代，productivity 提升从 20-50% 变成几乎没有上限。Agent swarm 并行铺开复杂工作，会在网络安全、生命科学以及任何可并行化计算机工作的领域带来质变的成果。他更深的观点是：我们的大脑并不适应"compute 成为可弹性伸缩的变量"这个世界，因为我们习惯认为投入的时间与产出相关。

**Latent Space: [One Million AI Engineer Special] How Stanford Teaches AI-Native Engineering — Mihail Eric, Monaco**

The Takeaway: A well-trained AI-native engineer is now one of the most valuable assets any company can hire, but the floor has risen so much that graduating students need to arrive closer to senior level than to entry level.

Mihail Eric is an adjunct professor at Stanford, where he created and now teaches CS 146, The Modern Software Developer, and he is also head of AI at Monaco, working on massive production codebases. That combination matters: the course is not a survey of tools but a practitioner's map of what actually holds up when you point agents at millions of lines of code. The curriculum, now in its second year, was rebuilt substantially because autonomous coding agents changed what is possible. "Most students are cooked, some of them are gonna be super valuable," he says, half-joking but clearly serious about the gap widening between juniors who learn to wield agents well and those who don't.

The course runs ten weeks: building a coding agent from scratch in about 200 lines, then context engineering at scale, tool and MCP design, skills as reusable procedures, agent configuration files and sub-agents, agent-ready codebases, agentic code review, security and new attack vectors, background agents triggered by events and CI/CD, and finally team-level infrastructure like LLM gateways and MCP portals, ending with the software factory and where humans still sit. His favorite teaching move is having students intercept Claude Code's actual requests with a proxy to read the real system prompts, plan prompts, and compaction prompts. "You can't... it's not 100% solidified. I mean, by design." The open source partnership component is what he's proudest of: students contribute to real AI-native repos, and maintainers review their code and pass on the instincts of good software design.

On context, his framework (research, proposal, implementation, testing) mirrors the idea that vanilla prompting does not scale to huge codebases. The hardest unsolved problem in his view is multi-agent context management: whether sub-agents inherit the main thread's context, how to consolidate what they return, and how to avoid over-constraining them with stale instructions. His advice for staying current is almost embarrassingly simple: experiment for ten minutes a day doing something you didn't do before.
