**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 97: Jev Creator on Decision Models, Benchmark Fatigue & Why the Labs Missed It**

The Takeaway: The labs have had enough raw intelligence to automate far more work than they have for years, and the real bottleneck is not benchmark scores but reliability, utility and a developer ecosystem with actual agency.

Jev's creator argues that the field is stuck in a "Stockholm syndrome" where developers plan around whatever model providers ship instead of pushing back on what they actually need. He calls the current state "disempowering and kind of lame," and says almost none of the real world feedback reaches his roadmap: "I think people should be complaining to us so much more." His favorite way to get a meeting is to complain about model jaggedness, not to talk business.

The core technical argument is about what shape intelligence gets compressed into. He believes the shape AI actually compresses to is very "system one" (fast, instinctive, mistake-prone), and that being honest about those weaknesses is more valuable than pretending a single intelligence axis exists. He points out the absurdity that models can attack Millennium Prize math problems but cannot automate anything touching accounting. RLVR is "incredible, but it is incredible at human in the loop stuff still."

He is sharply anti-benchmark. In his view, benchmark-optimized releases have burned developer goodwill so fast that people have already stopped caring about new releases in this model class, which is good for his company but bad for the field. He worries the wave of new "shapes" of models is overpromising and underdelivering, and that ML people are focusing on planting flags instead of utility: "I just really, really hope that people start giving, like, unbelievable levels of fucks for utility, usefulness, reliability."

The most interesting technical thread is his frustration with "the tyranny of the KV cache." Almost every UX decision in coding agents is built around an append-only stateful cache, which blocks routing, makes subagents awkward, forces lossy compaction, and prevents multiple agents from truly sharing context. He sketches sci-fi alternatives: semantic compaction that recomputes context per task, read-only agents writing live visualizers of what a coding agent is doing, and "semantic dependent typing" where a type system uses Jev-like models to verify natural-language properties of outputs.

On Anthropic's IPO he is skeptical of baked-in expectations, warning that "let them cure cancer" will become the modern "let them eat cake." He says Jev can run profitably and that he hopes never to temporarily subsidize for lock-in, calling it "a very shadowy, sneaky way to get people to use your service." His biggest changed mind in the past weeks: he is glad the team did not ship a benchmark-max version to test the market.

**Unsupervised Learning — Ep 97: Jev Creator on Decision Models, Benchmark Fatigue & Why the Labs Missed It**

核心要点：各大实验室多年来早已拥有足够的原始智能去自动化远超其实际成果的工作量，真正的瓶颈不是 benchmark 分数，而是可靠性、实用性，以及一个真正拥有主动权的开发者生态。

Jev 的创造者认为，整个行业陷入了某种"斯德哥尔摩综合征"：开发者围绕模型厂商发布的东西做规划，而不是反过来推动厂商去解决他们真正需要的问题。他称现状"令人丧失能动性，而且有点没劲"，并说几乎没有任何真实世界的反馈进入他们的路线图："我觉得大家应该多得多地来向我们抱怨。"他说让自己答应会面的最快方式，是抱怨模型的不平整性（jaggedness），而不是谈生意。

核心技术论点在于智能最终被压缩成什么形状。他认为 AI 实际被压缩成的形状非常"系统一"（快速、本能、容易出错），而诚实地承认这些弱点，比假装存在单一智能轴更有价值。他指出一个荒谬之处：模型能攻克千禧年数学大奖问题，却无法自动化任何涉及会计的工作。RLVR"非常了不起，但它在需要人类在环的事情上才了不起"。

他强烈反对 benchmark。在他看来，为 benchmark 优化的发布已经以极快的速度消耗了开发者的信任，人们已经不再关心这一类模型的新版本，这对他公司是好事，对整个领域却是坏事。他担心一波又一波新的模型"形状"正在过度承诺、交付不足，也担心 ML 圈的人只顾插旗而不是关注实用性："我真的、真的希望大家开始对实用性、有用性、可靠性给予难以置信的重视。"

最有意思的技术线索是他对"KV cache 的暴政"的不满。coding agent 几乎所有的 UX 决策都建立在一个只能追加的有状态 cache 之上，这阻碍了 routing，让 subagent 变得别扭，迫使压缩有损，也阻止多个 agent 真正共享上下文。他描绘了一些科幻式的替代方案：按任务重新计算上下文的语义压缩、由只读 agent 实时生成可视化来展示 coding agent 在做什么，以及"语义依赖类型"——让类型系统借助 Jev 类模型来验证输出的自然语言属性。

关于 Anthropic 的 IPO，他对已被计入价格的预期持怀疑态度，警告"让他们治愈癌症"会成为现代的"让他们吃蛋糕"。他说 Jev 可以盈利运营，并希望永远不必为了锁定用户而临时补贴，称那是"一种非常阴暗、偷偷摸摸的让人用你服务的方式"。他过去几周最大的改变想法是：他很庆幸团队没有发布一个 benchmark 拉满的版本来测试市场。
