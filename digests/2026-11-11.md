**Dan Shipper (CEO, Every)**

Dan Shipper laid out his Thesis conference argument: in the cracks of one week he did work that would have taken five people and $200,000, yet his team using the same models and same survey found completely different, non-default insights, because responsibility narrows where each person looks and perspective decides what matters. He cited an experiment where Claude predicted his Slack replies 93% of the time given 400 attempts but ranked his actual answer in the top 10% only around 60% of the time, arguing generation is cheap and ranking is hard. His conclusion: as models improve they raise the floor for everyone, so value comes from pushing past the default with your own perspective, and we should measure AI by how good it is at doing my work from my perspective.

Dan Shipper 阐述了他在 Thesis 大会上的核心论点：他在一周的碎片时间里完成了过去需要五个人、20 万美元才能做完的工作，但团队用同样的模型、同样的问卷却得出了完全不同的非默认洞察，因为责任范围决定了每个人往哪里看，而个人视角决定什么才重要。他引用了一个实验：给 Claude 400 次机会时它能 93% 命中他会在 Slack 说的话，但让它对自己的答案排序时，真正的答案只有约 60% 的概率进入前 10%，说明生成很便宜、排序很难。他的结论是：模型变强会抬高所有人的地板，因此价值来自用你自己的视角去突破默认答案，衡量 AI 的标准也该变成"它有多擅长从我的视角做我的工作"。

**AI & I by Every: A New Vision for Human Work After Automation**

The Takeaway: When everyone has access to the same model, the default answer becomes worthless, and the durable value is the perspective and responsibility a specific person brings to pushing the model past that default.

Dan Shipper, co-founder and CEO of Every, closed his company's first conference with an answer to the question the whole day was built around: what does great human work look like after automation? He frames prediction in two modes. The aggregate gathers lots of data and extrapolates a trend line, which is how we got scaling laws and how OpenAI's GDPval benchmark shows AI beating human experts 12.4% of the time in 2024, 47.6% in 2025, and around 83% in 2026. The intimate zooms into one messy real situation and extrapolates from there. Looking only at the aggregate, he admits, "you are like, we are so fucked."

Then he zooms in. In the cracks of one week he analyzed a customer survey, built a game for his team, spotted a teams-tier opportunity, vibe-coded a CRM, and started emailing customers, work that in 2020 terms would have taken a data scientist, game developer, marketer, engineer, and salesperson, roughly $200,000. Yet when he gave the same survey and same Claude to his team, everyone surfaced something different and non-obvious: one person found first-month churn driven by too much email, another found a loneliness psychographic among readers, another turned it into a Claymation game show.

His sharpest evidence: an experiment where Claude, given 400 attempts, predicted what he'd say in Slack 93% of the time, but when asked to rank its own answers, his real answer landed in the top 10% only around 60% of the time. Generation is cheap; ranking is hard. Responsibility narrows where a person looks, and perspective decides what is worth acting on. His answer to the obvious objection that models will get better at ranking: comparing 2026 Yash to 2027 Claude is rigged, because there will also be a 2027 Yash using 2027 Claude, and the model sits on both sides of the equation. "I pick Yash every day, every time."

Every 联合创始人兼 CEO Dan Shipper 在他公司首届大会的闭幕演讲中，回答了整场活动围绕的那个问题：自动化之后，伟大的人类工作长什么样？他把预测未来分成两种方式。一种是"聚合"（aggregate）：收集大量数据、外推一条趋势线，scaling law 就是这么来的，OpenAI 的 GDPval benchmark 也是这么显示 AI 在 2024 年只有 12.4% 的时候赢过人类专家，2025 年是 47.6%，2026 年已经到了约 83%。另一种是"亲密"（intimate）：放大到一个真实而混乱的具体场景，再从那里外推。他坦言，只看聚合视角，"你会觉得，我们完蛋了。"
