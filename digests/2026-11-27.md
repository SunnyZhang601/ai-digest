**Latent Space: Inside the AI Training Data Market — Gabe Greenberg, G2i**
The Takeaway: The human-data business for frontier labs is no longer about cheap labelers picking between two answers. It is a high-trust, high-ticket partnership business where the scarce skill is designing RL tasks that sit just a few steps beyond what the model can already do.

Gabe Greenberg is CEO of G2i, a company that supplies human data and computer-use environments to frontier labs, and co-runs AI Engineer conferences. He built the business with no VC money, going from about $15 million last year to roughly $100 million this year while profitable, and he thinks it could triple next year. That scale matters because it tells you how much of modern model progress is bought rather than invented in-house.

His sharpest insight is about task difficulty as a design discipline. "If you make them too hard, they become evals. And if you make them too easy, they're worthless. So you need to make them just hard enough that they're a few steps away from what the model can do today, so they're trainable, and efficiently trainable." That single constraint explains why the labor force shifted from thousands of mid-level gig engineers in 2023 to a much smaller pool of staff-level engineers doing taste work and complex dataset building.

The buyer side is the opposite of enterprise sales. There is no competitive RFP, and a researcher can spend $10 million in 60 seconds, so a pilot worth a few million dollars is pocket change that earns the right to the next big contract. What labs want has also shifted, from volume and raw correctness a year and a half ago to behavior, taste, and gap analysis now. Greenberg argues quality data is worth far more than a 20 percent price saving, and that labs mostly avoid building this in-house not because of money but because of leverage and legal exposure from managing thousands of individual contributors.

His forward view: robotics, 3D world models, CAD, mechanical engineering, and chip design are the next frontiers, and six-month contracts are still signable there, unlike in coding where that timeline now gets laughed at.

Latent Space：Inside the AI Training Data Market — Gabe Greenberg, G2i
核心要点：面向前沿实验室的人类数据生意，已经不再是廉价标注员在两个答案之间做选择。它变成了一门高信任、高客单价的合作生意，稀缺能力在于设计出刚好比模型现有能力往前几步的 RL 任务。

Gabe Greenberg 是 G2i 的 CEO，公司为前沿实验室提供人类数据和 computer use 环境，同时他还联合主办 AI Engineer 大会。他在没有 VC 投资的情况下把生意做了起来，从去年约 1500 万美元增长到今年约 1 亿美元并且盈利，他认为明年还可能翻三倍。这个规模之所以重要，是因为它说明了现代模型进步中有多大一部分是"买来的"，而不是实验室内部发明的。

他最锋利的洞察是把任务难度当作一门设计 discipline："如果做得太难，它们就变成 evals 了。如果做得太简单，就毫无价值。所以你得做到刚好够难，比模型今天能做的往前几步，这样才是可训练的，而且是高效可训练的。"这一条约束解释了为什么劳动力结构从 2023 年的数千名中级零工工程师，转向了规模小得多、做品味工作和复杂数据集构建的 staff 级工程师。

买方这边的逻辑和企业销售完全相反。没有竞标 RFP，一个研究员 60 秒就能花掉 1000 万美元，所以几百万美元的试点只是零钱，用来换取下一个大合同的机会。实验室想要的东西也变了：一年半前要的是量和原始正确性，现在要的是行为、品味和 gap 分析。Greenberg 认为高质量数据的价值远高于省下 20% 的价格，而实验室大多不自建这套能力，原因不是钱，而是管理数千名个体贡献者带来的杠杆和法律风险。

他对未来的判断是：机器人、3D 世界模型、CAD、机械工程和芯片设计是接下来的前沿，而且在这些领域今天还能签六个月的合同，而在 coding 领域提六个月已经会被人笑话。
